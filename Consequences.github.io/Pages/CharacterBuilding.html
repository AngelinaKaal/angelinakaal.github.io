Base Skill Level

Each character begins at a “measly” level one. Though granted a few boons by the gods and spirits, your character is still a fraction of the powerhouse that they can become.

After many trials and tribulations you may reach the peak of your power at level 50, where nearly all mundane tasks are effortless and only the mightiest of foes may dare challenge you.

Whenever you roll a dice your base skill level is added, it starts at 1 and goes up 1 point each 10 levels, representing your overall increase in power, insight, speed and prowess.



How does combat work?

This game isn’t designed with battlemaps in mind, so there are no exact measurements or distances one can go. My best advice to people who insist on using maps is to use other systems maps and distances like Pathfinder ect. For those who work without maps, use common sense and what seems reasonable and fun for everyone.

Once combat starts, everyone rolls initiative to determine the order. The initiative skill boosts your chance to be first. On your turn you can attack once. To attack someone, you use either: Melee, Ranged or a magic attack. The attack is rolled with the die your rank allows (see chapter 9). The enemy can try to dodge using their allowed die. Whoever has the higher number wins. On a success you do your damage as described in the weapons section, if you tie, the attacker does half damage, if the dodge roll is higher, you do no damage. items like shields and parry daggers make it harder for your enemies to hit you, while armor negates a percentage of the damage. Some abilities allow you to soak damage, which is entirely negated.

After 3 turns there should be a clear indication of who is winning, if the npc’s are losing, they will turn tail and flee, if the players are losing, an offer of surrender will be accepted, where the players will lose some items as they are taken prisoner.

The conflict handling skill allows players (or npc’s) to refuse to surrender for some turns until they win, or lose heart, as well as rerolling dice if the outcome is not desired. This can be done one reroll per day per rank.



Character sheet explanation

It is best you download the sheet and keep it open in another tab or print it out so you can reference it as you go. Here we will go over the character sheet’s first (and most important) page. It is cut into three parts. An overview of who and/or what your character is on the top. The middle part deals with the more mundane abilities. The last part is most important during combat, it holds your current hit points, the damage you do and other magical effects that are on you.

The top row is for your name as a player, the row below that holds your character name.

The third row is for your level and the base points. Both can be tallied if so desired.

Currency deals with how many coins you have of each type. Unless otherwise stated, for quality of life reasons the entire world deals with the same type and value of currency.

Under the “magical ability” row you fill in what your character is and can do. As stated before, your species and form are considered “magical” for this purpose.

In most games you will start out with 4 magical abilities. But always ask your DM first.

You may mix and match different species to become the ultimate crossbreed without issue. Do note however that some forms and species come with their own maluses as well as bonuses.

You unlock more magic abilities as you level up at level 10, 20 and 30. It is also possible to gain extra magical abilities through forms and species. When you “unlock” a power that you already possess, then this power isn’t further bolstered outside of your normal leveling.

These magical abilities are very broad and players are encouraged to mix and match them in creative ways, explain or describe them in their own unique way. The description given is merely a suggestion.

The magical item is the single item gifted at the start of the game and it does not “run out of charges” unlike all other items in the game, this makes them uniquely powerful.

Below that are the rows of Melee, ranged and magical damage with their own calculations.

There may be extra damage added through magical abilities, but this is for the player to keep track of. At a baseline, the weapons do their own damage + the character level.

Magic however is tied to the “energy level” skill both for its “accuracy” as well as the damage it deals. The second column is where you can write down what dice you can use D4 to 2D20.

Hitpoints follow a similar build up with a baseline of 5 hitpoints per level, + extra hit point per rank in condition. Again, various magical abilities may increase hitpoints per level, or give a singular boost.

Armour is divided in light (¼) and medium and heavy (½). This means that when wearing armour, either a quarter, or half of the damage is negated, before it comes into contact with your soak. Soak represents a tough hide, scales, or just being built differently. And it negates an X amount of damage regardless of armour. Soak is always “last” in the equation of battle.

At the bottom is a field where you can write down extra effects that you want to keep track of, immunities, resistances, and other powers not otherwise noted.

On the last pages is an empty character sheet that is fillable.

Another short explanation on how the character sheet works:

Base points: [bt]This starts out as 1 and goes up by one every 10 levels. These points are added after you roll your die.

Magic abilities[bu] These are your special powers but also the races and “forms”. It is allowed and encouraged to mix and match, you cannot however take one power twice.

Armor and Soak: Armour reduces the damage and comes first in the calculation. Soak is the amount of damage that is ignored by either thick hide, magic belts or items. In the armor box, choose which is applicable, ½ or ¼.

Weapon damage: is[bv] always a fixed number, you never roll for attack, it is base damage + your level. You only roll to hit someone, and they can dodge (if they see you coming).

Hitpoints[bw]: 5*level, +1 hitpoint for each rank in the condition skill, the condition skill works retroactive for ease of calculation

Servants: They can be hired to maintain your house, shop, castle or cart, t[by]hey usually cost some coins but can make your life a lot easier.

Level 45/50: You can also find two “Perfect” skills at level 45 and 50, these are any non-combat skills that you will no longer have to roll for, [bz]once you reach that pinnacle of power, you always get the maximum positive outcome. In this case you can see that a character can possibly start with a skill in the S+-tier

The last page is pretty straight forward:

Items: This is your inventory that you carry with you on your journeys

Assets and Titles[cb] are usually granted by nobility, royalty, clergy, guildmasters and other people of power. These are cottages, houses, armouries, but also titles and letters of the mark. These can help with social interactions, grant access to resources or give players a place to live and receive guests.

To keep accounting to a minimum, it is strongly advised not to bother with upkeep and payments of the various estates and narrate that these pay for themselves through whichever means seems logical.

Retainers: These are the people who follow you through payment or through titles, they can be manservants, guards, cooks, hairdressers, maids, spies or mages. They happen usually later in the game when your character is becoming more of a political or public entity, rather than a scruffy pigherder[ce] who happens to have a magic sword. They are usually granted through domains or titles and are paid in such a way. They are normally nowhere near as powerful as the player characters, but can have skills that the character doesn’t have. A captain for a boat, a physician for medical issues and other such things.



Ability selection

The ability section deals with the skills everyone can possess to a greater or lesser degree. Anyone can hit someone else, try to get a lower price through haggling or be good at maths (Beta lore). You can also find two “Perfect” skills at level 45 and 50, these are any non-combat skill that you will no longer have to roll for, once you reach that pinnacle of power, you always get the maximum positive outcome. In this case you can see that a character can possibly start with a skill in the S+-tier.



Reaching S+ tier and over

Once a character has reached S+ in a skill they roll with 2D20’s, but what happens if you gain more ranks in that skill, will it be 3D20?

No, instead the character will gain an additional basepoint to that roll, each increase in that ability will increase the basepoint. That way you will never have to worry about “losing out” on maxed out skills and abilities.

This is the ultimate example of:

“Training until you do it right VS. Training until you can’t do it wrong.”
