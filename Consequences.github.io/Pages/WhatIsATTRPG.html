Consequences

What is a TTRPG?

A tabletop roleplaying game (TTRPG) is a game where you and your friends and/or family, or even strangers, join together to tell a story. These stories often take place in wondrous and magical places. In this system one of you is the GM (game master) who directs the story. All the other people play a character in this story, usually as heroes, but there is a place for villains as well.

The GM usually prepares the story where the other players have their characters act in. This game is very collaborative, this isn’t a game where the Gamemaster tries to “win”, nor is this a game where the players win by scoring points or resources in the story. It inherently is a story that you tell together.

After everyone has created their character a GM might start of the story. He/she describes a mighty fortress in the deep dark night, with only a few starts above. The cold wind blows through leafless trees as a lone guard on the wall huddles against the winterwind. In the main hall of this fortress runes glow upon the ground as the high mage as well as the high priest conduct their ritual. The ritual is to summon mighty heroes from a faraway place to save them from an invasion of magical beasts who plunder the land…

And so the GM describes how the players characters start as the perceived heroes of this realm. The players can only manipulate their own characters. The GM works the world around the player characters and describes what the players may encounter, see or interact with in the game world. If a player character wants to steal an apple, the GM can ask the player to roll a dice according to their skill, to see if they succeed in stealing the apple.

Some important rules for people who have never played a TTRPG before.

The main goal of the game is to have fun. Both for you as for the other players. you are supposed to have fun, but so do other players. Don’t impede on their play or make the game more difficult for them than you need to. Having conflict between characters is alright, purposefully ruining a game is not.

Only roll dice when asked by your GM. Some actions may not require a roll, like opening a door, putting on your boots or even riding a horse. Other times you don’t need to roll because what you are trying is impossible. Convincing the king to hand over the crown because you are scary, isn’t likely going to work. Having your character die or having a setback isn’t the same as you losing, or that you have failed as a player. We are telling stories, and not all stories end with the hero getting the girl. sometimes the story ends with a mishap on the battlefield. No problem you can just make a new character and join again.

Doing voices is optional, this goes for both GMs and players alike. There are masterful voice actors who can do tons of accents and dialects. If you can’t do voices, then don’t force yourself. You are playing for fun after all.

What concepts and ideas are in the game?

The main concepts in this game is not inherently the struggle for survival or the battle to become more powerful, but what that increase in power does to you and the world. A level 1 character is like most other adventurers, men at arms or residents of a city. But soon your power will grow, and with that your fame, your wealth and your influence.

When you are stronger than most soldiers, scarier than most bandits or wealthier than your average merchant, people will take notice of you. They will want to ally with you, they may offer a position in an organization or even ask you to set up a local branch.

Once you reach even greater levels of power, you may be elevated into higher ranks. You will certainly get foes who are jealous of your quick rise, of your power and of what you can do.

The great lords and ladies of the nation, and perhaps other nations, may start to vie for your favor, wanting to bind you to them by giving you lands, titles or perhaps a marriage. Still more will want what you have. And your life will change from merry adventure to perhaps politics, war…or you may retain your freedom and independence, being feared by many in high places for what you could do should you turn against them.

In the end, every action and choice has consequences.

The game starts with all the characters being gathered in a place none have been before. Congratulations, you have all been whisked away (or died) and will be brought to a new world. A place haunted by a demon king, by a calamity or perhaps just a calm and peaceful world, as a reward for being an upstanding good person. Regardless of the reason, the world you go to is a world of magic and mythical beings. Which form it takes exactly is up to you and your table.

Before you stands a spirit or deity of some kind. They will inform you that you aren’t going empty handed. No indeed you get to choose your very own skills and abilities, much like in a video game. And on top of that, you get to pick your very own magic item to help you on your way!

What are my options?

So what are your options to play?

Worry not reïncarnist, you can choose to start out as a non human, an elf, dwarf or something more mythical. Each option has their own benefits and their own specialty.

You can play a hero, a villain or something in between, however this should always be agreed upon with the GM and other players before you start. It usually isn’t fun to fight between the players from the get go.

As the writer of this system I advise to have a prep-session with all players and the GM on general expectations, hopes and dislikes. In the end any story can be as juvenile, horrifically dark or amazing as the group wants it to be.
