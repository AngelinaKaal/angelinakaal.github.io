Consequences

What is the game world like?

This is fully open to both the players and GM to decide, inspiration can be gathered from many places and the tone may

vary depending on the type of story that will be told. The game lends itself mostly for fantasy of course. But if you

want to run a “modern day world with elves and dwarves” that is totally up to the table. Be free, be creative

Base Skill Level

Each character begins at a “measly” level one. Though granted a few boons by the gods and spirits, your character is still a fraction of the powerhouse that they can become.

After many trials and tribulations you may reach the peak of your power at level 50, where nearly all mundane tasks are effortless and only the mightiest of foes may dare challenge you.

Whenever you roll a dice your base skill level is added, it starts at 1 and goes up 1 point each 10 levels, representing your overall increase in power, insight, speed and prowess.

Magic in the World

Magic in this system and setting can be many things, my goal is to leave it open for interpretation. For flavor sake I would say that any system goes as long as it doesn't interfere with the mechanics. If you decide your character needs a special stone, wand, herb or incantation that is all fine and legitimate. In practice you could cast many spells or perform many acts of magic throughout the day, although for the narrative it would make sense if you do get tired from it, like one gets tired from running and working out. In the chapter of magic abilities you will find various elemental magics and some others. But how many people know magic in the world?

The following is an example of what you could reasonably find in various settlements:
  • A small village may have a low level alchemist and/or witch.(D-rank)
  • A large village may have an arcane smith or wonder worker, and a witch/alchemist (D-rank)
  • A small town may hold the above but also a C-rank magical healer
  • A large town may hold the above and 2 D-rank other powered people
  • A city of city fortress will hold at least 2 magic shops, a court which houses a few magic inclined people(max B-rank in 2 abilities.)
  • A capital will hold up to 4 magic shops, some decently competent mages (Max A-rank 1 in ability) of “imperial” level. There are no known S-rank mages other than potential characters.


Surrendering enemies and fleeing foes

When people engage in a fight, some death and wounds are bound to happen. But only rarely do bandits fight till death, w[bb]olves don’t keep attacking if they see no chance of winning and if an army loses a third of their troops, they will likely rout.

A victory for the players doesn’t always have to end with endless fields of corpses, p[bc]layers can choose not to murder all bandits and wild animals they come across, and you as[bd] GM can encourage them not to go down an extremely dark path.

Let enemies surrender after the 3 rounds of battle if they aren’t winning. Some may flee and others may just lay down their arms.

Wild animals and magical creatures like dragons and the like will flee before they are killed. When you do this, make sure to let your players know that they didn’t “fail to kill” the enemy, but succeeded in defeating them.

Bandits can be turned in for bounties, dragons leave their hoard and routing armies will drop massive amounts of supplies, weapons and armor.



Large battles or skirmishes

Speaking of armies and massive battles. When the characters are all level 50 and there isn’t any single individual that can oppose them, it may or may not occur that they are thrust into battle on behalf of a king, emperor, pope, general or other leader of an organization. Or perhaps they want to protect the village they live in from a horde of undead.

Even with battle maps it would take ages to go through all individual forces to attack, see how much damage is done, calculate their hitpoints and so on. I advise[be] GM’s[bf] to divide a large-scale battle into a smaller number of skirmishes. The players can choose to attack the vanguard, the rear guard, the supplies or the flanks of the army that they wish to attack.

Each side could be divided again into 3 separate skirmishes. So in total, defeating an entire army would take about 9 separate fights.

Keeping in mind that it is human nature and historical evidence that when an army loses about 30% of their fighting force, the rest of that group will rout,[bg] the players won’t need to completely lay waste to the enemy forces.

While it is manageable to work with stronger opponents in one-on-one fights, like a captain, a handful of knights or perhaps some monsters that are brought to bear.[bh] Fighting thousands of “minion level” enemies just takes too long. A wise GM would then merge the entire enemy force into a “swarm” where attacks deal total damage to the whole group rather than individuals. Especially on higher levels this makes sense. You can also choose to have a smaller swarm and a few higher leveled enemies who as mentioned can be knights or commanders for a bit less “faceless enemies” and give your players something to aim at
